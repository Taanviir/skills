Project overview · v0.4

Tidepool

Tide gauge readings and depth forecasts for small ports that cannot afford a commercial tide service.

Piloting at Port Calloway and Westhaven since January 2026
What it delivers

Know the water depth at every berth before the ship arrives

Gauge sync

Collects readings from up to 40 coastal gauges over the public NMEA feed, every 6 minutes.

Tide prediction

A tide model per gauge, fitted from 30 days of readings and refreshed every night.

Port dashboard

One web page per port showing predicted depth for each berth over the next 48 hours.

Low-water alerts · in progress

Will text the harbor master 12 hours ahead when a ship's margin drops under 0.5 m. Waiting on the SMS contract.

How it works

Four small services on one local database

Collect
Poll gauges every 5 min, store readings in SQLite
→
Model
Refit each gauge's tide model nightly
→
Check
Compare ship drafts with predicted depth every 15 min
→
Show
Dashboard with 48 h depth per berth
  • No missed readings — polling runs slightly faster than the gauges report.
  • Cheap to run — public gauge feed, a single SQLite file, no paid data service.
  • Low-water flags already logged — each one is written to a log file until SMS is switched on.
The headline feature

Depth forecasts within 8 cm, 95% of the time

  • Built from the port's own gauges — a standard harmonic tide model per gauge, using five main tidal components.
  • Learns from 30 days of data — no historical archive or survey needed to get started.
  • Stays current — the model is refitted every night.
  • Proven at two ports — accuracy measured against observed water levels at Port Calloway and Westhaven.

Why this matters

A grounded ship is costly and dangerous. Small ports usually go without forecasts because commercial tide services cost too much. Tidepool gives harbor crews berth-level depth forecasts from free public data, and the same forecasts will drive the SMS warnings.
